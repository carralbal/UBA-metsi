# METSI content architecture

## Source layer

Preserve the complete reading as ordered, addressable blocks. The editorial plan may distribute one conceptual section across several pages or combine tightly related short sections, but it may not delete, paraphrase, or replace eligible source text when exact preservation is required.

## Publication sequence

Adapt this sequence to the class; omit unsupported elements.

1. Editorial cover: class label, discipline, title, short thesis.
2. Opening spread: professional question, thesis, and first operational tension.
3. Conceptual foundation: definitions, distinctions, and boundary conditions.
4. System view: relationships, actors, flows, evidence, or dependencies.
5. Application: rule, worked example, counterexample, or decision test.
6. Method in use: procedure, tailoring, risks, authority, and traceability.
7. Case universe: Hotel Horizonte or class-specific operational setting.
8. Professional consequences: limits, tensions, governance, and AI implications.
9. Transfer: a second context that tests whether the concept generalizes.
10. Synthesis and preparation questions.
11. References and reading route.
12. Image-only close when part of the approved series.

## Density and rhythm

- One dominant claim per page, but supporting claims may share a spread.
- Plan by six-column page and twelve-column spread rather than card count.
- Ordinary body pages may use two or three columns; a narrow rail may carry evidence, portrait, note, or image sequence.
- Do not reduce the normal body size to absorb overflow. Continue the article or choose another layout family.
- No ordinary page may contain more than 40% avoidable empty area.
- Introduce an intentional visual pause every four to six pages.
- Avoid more than two consecutive text-dominant pages or two consecutive pages from the same family.
- A full N-document of 16 pages or more must use at least five composition families.

## Editorial transformations

Safe when source fidelity permits: headings, chunking, drop caps, pull quotes, concise transitions, turning true parallel structures into tables or diagrams, and adding source-grounded worked examples.

Require explicit care: changing terminology, compressing definitions, merging claims from different sections, manufacturing captions as quotations, or adding facts. Distinguish all editorial additions from the source manifest.
