# METSI conceptual depth standard

This reference is a blocking gate for every complete N-reading. It prevents a long, visually polished document from passing when its conceptual treatment is thin, repetitive, fragmented, or pedagogically abrupt.

## Audience and progression

Write for undergraduate readers who are usually 18–20 years old, have limited prior Systems knowledge, and have studied some Software Engineering. Each reading must move in this order:

1. an accessible professional question and a substantial story, analogy, or scene;
2. a plain-language explanation of the object under study;
3. distinctions from adjacent concepts already known from Software Engineering;
4. mechanisms, trade-offs, boundary conditions, and evidence;
5. technical and academic depth;
6. application to Hotel Horizonte and at least one transfer context;
7. synthesis, memorable pills, glossary, and preparation questions.

Do not begin with taxonomy, jargon, or a dense theoretical catalogue. Do not remain at the introductory level after the opening.

## Substantive word count

When the course standard calls for 6,000 or more words, count only substantive conceptual body. Exclude:

- cover and publication metadata;
- contents and referents;
- captions and visual microcopy;
- repeated Hotel Horizonte character cards;
- five pills;
- glossary;
- preparation questions;
- bibliography and links;
- visual instructions or production notes.

The minimum is a floor, not a target. A document with 6,000 words still fails if it repeats, fragments, or pads.

## Complete concept units

Every major concept must answer all six questions:

1. **Definition:** What exactly is it?
2. **Distinction:** What neighboring idea is it commonly confused with?
3. **Mechanism:** How and why does it produce an effect?
4. **Evidence:** What would strengthen or weaken the claim?
5. **Example:** How does it operate in a concrete episode?
6. **Boundary or consequence:** When does it fail, and what decision changes?

A heading followed by one definition and one generic example is not a developed concept. Combine or expand thin sections rather than accumulating headings.

## Required explanatory variety

For a full N-reading, include at minimum:

- one substantial opening story that occupies approximately one magazine page after the professional question;
- three or more theoretical lenses or traditions, integrated and contrasted rather than name-dropped;
- six or more worked examples distributed through the reading: at least two simple examples, two Hotel Horizonte episodes or decisions, one counterexample, and one transfer outside the hotel;
- two or more explicit rival explanations, trade-offs, or controversies;
- one operational method, decision test, or protocol that a student can apply;
- one section on recurrent errors or misconceptions;
- one current 2026 implication when materially relevant.

Examples must change the reader's understanding or decision. Decorative anecdotes and one-sentence illustrations do not count.

## Literature and currency

- Use foundational references for the intellectual lineage.
- Add recent publications from 2022 onward, preferably 2024 onward where available.
- Connect each major theoretical lens to claims in the body.
- Distinguish established knowledge, current debate, professional guidance, and authorial synthesis.
- Do not present a bibliography as evidence that was never used in the explanation.

## Cross-document fit

Before drafting, state privately:

- prerequisite concepts this N may assume;
- the unique conceptual advance of this N;
- concepts deliberately deferred to later N-documents;
- potential duplication with the preceding and following readings.

Read the immediate predecessor and successor plans or sources. Repetition is allowed only as a short bridge that produces a new inference.

## Fragmentation and padding blockers

Reject the source before design if any is true:

- the minimum word count is reached mainly through glossary, bibliography, lists, summaries, or repeated case material;
- more than half of major sections contain fewer than 150 substantive words without a deliberate editorial reason;
- a later "second layer" repeats earlier sections instead of deepening them;
- several headings restate the same claim with new labels;
- lists replace causal explanation;
- examples merely paraphrase the preceding sentence;
- a current AI section is generic and could be pasted into another N unchanged;
- the Hotel Horizonte case does not alter or test the conceptual argument;
- a reader can repeat definitions but cannot explain what evidence or decision follows.

## Human depth audit

Score each dimension 0–4:

1. conceptual precision;
2. causal or mechanism-based explanation;
3. progression from accessible to technical;
4. worked examples and counterexamples;
5. literature integration and 2026 currency;
6. Hotel Horizonte application;
7. transfer and boundary conditions;
8. cross-document uniqueness;
9. readability for the target audience;
10. decision usefulness.

Blocking threshold: no dimension below 3 and at least 35/40 overall. Record one sentence of evidence for every score. If the source fails, rewrite it before image curation, infographics, or composition.

## Red-team questions

Before declaring content ready, answer:

- Which three ideas would a student misunderstand if they only skimmed the headings?
- Where does the text explain *why*, not merely *what*?
- Which example could falsify or limit the main thesis?
- What is genuinely new here relative to the previous N?
- Which recent source changes or complicates the foundational account?
- What concrete professional decision can a student make better after reading?

If those answers are weak or generic, the N-document is not content-ready.
