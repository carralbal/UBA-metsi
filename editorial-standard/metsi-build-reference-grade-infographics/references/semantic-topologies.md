# Semantic topology families

Choose topology from meaning. Vary compositions across a document and across classes.

## 1. System or ecosystem map

Use for interacting people, technology, processes, organizational structures, channels, and external services.

- Compose several distinct clusters around shared exchange points.
- Show boundaries, ports, and interfaces explicitly.
- Use axonometric stacks for layered technology and small plates for processes or policies.
- Prefer routed dotted connectors with visible endpoints.

## 2. Evidence assembly

Use when heterogeneous inputs become a defensible body of evidence.

- Place sources in a horizontal or arced intake field.
- Distinguish events, source of truth, raw data, rules, observations, and contradictions.
- Route each input into an open axonometric container or evidence object.
- Show validation or transformation before synthesis when the source requires it.

## 3. Decision and governance atlas

Use for participation, authority, evidence rights, accountability, escalation, repair, and veto.

- Separate who knows, who decides, who executes, who is affected, and who repairs.
- Use lanes, levels, or clustered roles instead of interchangeable cards.
- Make missing authority or control gaps visible through open routes or unmatched ports.

## 4. Process-service map

Use for end-to-end service journeys and socio-technical flows.

- Show sequential tasks without implying that the process is purely linear.
- Attach actors, systems, policies, evidence, and exceptions at the points where they matter.
- Use feedback routes for rework, learning, or escalation.

## 5. Layered architecture

Use for stacked distinctions, infrastructures, data layers, methods, rules, or organizational levels.

- Use axonometric slabs whose ordering has explicit meaning.
- Connect external labels with short leaders and dedicated text lanes.
- Do not put long text on the faces of small objects.

## 6. Multi-panel editorial overview

Use for a compact synthesis of several related but distinct mechanisms.

- Use two to six panels with different internal micro-diagrams.
- Maintain a shared visual grammar while letting each panel use its own topology.
- Link panels only when the relation is meaningful.
- Avoid a generic dashboard of equal cards.

## Hybrid selection

A hybrid is appropriate when the central claim combines, for example, a layered system with a process flow and governance roles. The hybrid must still have one dominant reading path.

Do not use the same topology three times in one document unless the comparison itself requires structural repetition.
